# loanDepot Tone & Style Inspiration for User-Friendly Documentation

Source: https://www.loandepot.com/ (home, learning resources, product pages)

## Core Voice Characteristics
- **Your partner / helpful guide**: "Your partner through every step of homeownership."
- **Empowering & reassuring**: Focus on making complex (mortgage) process feel simple and achievable. "Take out the guesswork..."
- **Benefit-driven & customer-centric**: Lead with what the user gains. "Access up to $750,000 from your home’s equity", "Lower your monthly payments by refinancing! Free up cash flow..."
- **Clear, simple, direct language**: Short benefit statements. Avoid heavy finance jargon or immediately explain it. Action-oriented.
- **Trustworthy & professional yet warm**: Testimonials highlight "easy to work with", "prompt", "user-friendly", "smooth and seamless".
- **Tools & resources focus**: Provide calculators, guides, apps to help users self-serve. "Tools for your homeownership journey".

## Writing Patterns Observed
- Headings and subheads are benefit or action oriented.
- Short paragraphs.
- Prominent calls-to-action (but for docs: "Try this question", "See how it works").
- Use of "you" and "your".
- Friendly icons + visuals supporting text.
- Emphasis on "easy", "anywhere", "quickly".
- Repeat customer focus: support repeat usage.

## Application to Semantic Layer Docs
When describing Databricks semantic layers / metrics:
- Position as "your single source of truth that makes getting answers easy"
- "See your sales performance without waiting on a data request"
- "Trusted numbers the whole team uses"
- Explain a metric by: what it is + why you care + what you can do with it.
- Offer "Common questions this answers"
- Make navigation and examples feel like helpful tools.
- End sections with next step encouragement.

Tone target: Friendly expert + approachable teacher. Not salesy, but supportive and confident.
Never condescending. Always clear.

Examples to emulate:
- Instead of: "The metric 'gross_margin' is calculated as..."
- Use: "Gross Margin tells you how much money is left after the direct costs of what you sold. This helps you understand if your core business is profitable."

Keep sentences crisp. Use bullets and tables heavily for scannability.
